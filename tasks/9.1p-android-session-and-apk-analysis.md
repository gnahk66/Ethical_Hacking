## Objective
Investigate the exposure created by a deliberately installed lab APK and review the permissions/components declaring its capabilities.

## Task B1: controlled session
Recorded an Android Meterpreter session, the device address and current remote directory. Navigated shared storage and listed running processes. This establishes capabilities in the lab environment, not unrestricted access to every app's private data.

## Task B2: impact analysis
Compared storage access, process enumeration and network-position information. Storage may contain sensitive user material, while process and addressing details assist assessment. App-private data access depends on OS version, sandbox boundaries, granted privileges and device configuration.

## Task B3: APK review
The extracted manifest identified `com.metasploit.stage`, application components, an exported service and a receiver for boot completion. The submitted manifest listed minimum SDK 10 and target SDK 17, plus permissions related to SMS, contacts, camera, microphone, calls, location and storage.

These declarations show requested capabilities and entry points. They do not prove that every permission was granted, every sensor was used, or that persistence succeeded. Runtime restrictions differ across Android versions. Signing metadata also identifies a signing certificate; it does not independently prove the real-world author is trustworthy.

## Defensive recommendations
Restrict untrusted installation, review disproportionate permissions, keep devices supported and investigate unexpected background/network behaviour. Review exported components and enforce permissions at their entry points.

## Portfolio treatment
No APK, device screenshots containing user data or remote-control payloads are distributed. The repeated social-engineering summary in the source is not treated as additional mobile-specific work.
