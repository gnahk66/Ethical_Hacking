## Objective
Gather target information and compare the meaning of different discovery and scanning results.

## Method and observations
- **B1, username reconnaissance:** compared candidate social accounts. Similar names, avatars and posting patterns were leads rather than identity proof. Identifying account links are omitted here.
- **B2, banner inspection:** used ZAP to inspect an Apache response exposing versions of Apache, PHP, OpenSSL and additional modules. These details narrowed vulnerability research.
- **B3-B4, host discovery:** compared live and non-responsive lab addresses and tried different probe types, including ICMP timestamp discovery.
- **B5, port scanning:** compared full TCP connect, SYN, Xmas, ACK and UDP techniques.
- **B6, traffic fingerprinting:** examined TTL and TCP window values in packets from Linux and Windows targets.

## Interpretation
A TCP connect scan completes a connection; a SYN scan can stop after SYN/ACK. Both can be detected. ACK scanning informs filtering behaviour rather than whether an application is listening. Xmas results depend on TCP stack behaviour. UDP silence can mean open or filtered.

TTL and window sizes are clues, not conclusive operating-system identification. Routing changes TTL, and configurations can overlap. A verbose banner is not proof that every advertised component is exploitable; the Last-Modified header is not reliable evidence of patch age.

## Defensive recommendations
Reduce unnecessary version disclosure, maintain supported software, restrict exposed services and monitor unusual scan patterns. Do not substitute banner suppression for patching.
